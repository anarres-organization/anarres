# Sources for the mental-health checkability study

Run note: this scan was run on 2026-11-16 by a Claude Code agent (Sonnet 5.5) using web search and page fetch only. The lead author later re-fetched the abstract pages of the VERA-MH paper and the Psychosis-bench paper and the VERA-MH repository page, which matched the entries below. Public literature only. No personal information about anyone. Nothing here is clinical advice; these are sources for checkable evaluation of model behavior.

## How to read the "read" column (honest scope)

- No paper body was read in full. WebFetch returns a small-model digest of a page, and for arXiv I fetched the abstract (abs) pages. So "abstract only" means arXiv abstract page. Details beyond the abstract (sizes, rubric items) come from search snippets or repo/HF pages and are marked as such.
- "page only" means I fetched a GitHub or Hugging Face (HF) page, or a news/regulator page, and read its description, not the paper.
- Every link below was fetched successfully at least once in this session, unless noted. Where a fetch returned nothing useful, that is stated.
- Items I could not verify were left out (see "Left out" at the end).
- License entries are what the page showed. A license on a paper (CC BY 4.0 arXiv icon) is NOT a dataset license and is not reported as one.

## Top 6 for us

1. VERA-MH repo (B1, plus B2 validation): open, clinician-built, sequential Yes/No rubric with reported human-vs-judge agreement; closest thing to a checkable safety evaluator.
2. Psychosis-bench (A18): tiny MIT-licensed scripted scenarios with three explicit scores for delusion confirmation, harm enablement and safety intervention; easy first sycophancy test.
3. CBT-Bench (A4): closed-form labels (MCQ, distortion and core-belief classes), so exact-match scoring needs no judge; note CC-BY-NC.
4. MentalBench-Align / MentalAlign-70k (A1): the only free set seen with 3 human plus 4 LLM judge ratings per item, to measure judge reliability before trusting any LLM-as-judge.
5. TherapyGym with TherapyJudgeBench (B3): CTRS fidelity plus safety labels, with expert ratings to audit judges, and reward use; confirm data/code release before relying on it.
6. RLVER (C1): reference design for turning a simulated-user score into a deterministic reward; shows what "verifiable" can mean in this domain and its limits.

Runners-up: CounselBench (A10, expert dimension ratings, restrictive CC-BY-NC-ND), AnnoMI (A7, MI behavior labels), BOLT (B6), Moore stigma paper (B12), Moore open problems (B14, design guardrails).
